🎯 The Txotx Ritual

  • Txotx! — The call to the barrel. Stand, hold glass at angle, catch thin stream of cider to aerate (eskanziatu)
  • Drink immediately in one go (txinparta) — don't let it sit
  • Return to table for next course; repeat throughout meal

🍽️ Traditional Menu

  • Chorizo a la sidra (chorizo cooked in cider)
  • Tortilla de bacalao (salt cod omelet)
  • Txuleta (massive bone-in ribeye for meat-eaters)
  • Idiazabal cheese with walnuts & quince paste
Dress Code: Wear old shoes (floors are sticky with cider) and bring layers (cellars are cold, ~12°C). Standing room at traditional venues (Zapiain); seated at modern ones (Petritegi, Barkaiztegi).

🌿 Unique Parral Vine System

Unlike most wine regions, Basque vines use parral — elevated canopies on pergolas that improve airflow in the humid Atlantic climate, reducing fungal disease and creating a distinctive microclimate.

🍾 What to Look For

  • Hondarrabi Zuri — Primary white grape, high acidity, slight spritz
  • Hondarrabi Beltza — Rare red grape, light rosé/red styles
  • Sparkling Txakoli — Traditional method (Byhur at Astarbe)

🎪 Txosna Culture (Aste Nagusia)

Pop-Up Bars Run by Local Clubs Aug 22–30 only
Location
Along Arenal & Ribera quays (riverside)
Hours
~13:00–03:00 daily during festival
Distance from Calle García Salazar
~1 km / 4 min drive / 12 min walk
Transport
Metro Casco Viejo (L1, L2, L3) 2 min walk; walkable from centre
  • Each txosna is run by a comparsa (local social club/peña) — non-profit, community-funded
  • Cheap drinks (beer ~€2, kalimotxo ~€2.50), all profits fund club activities year-round
  • Each has distinct vibe: some traditional, some youth/party, some family-friendly
  • Communal tables — sit anywhere, chat with strangers, dance in the street
  • Food: pintxos, sandwiches, talo con chistorra (corn tortilla with chorizo)
  • No tickets/passes — just show up. Peak: 20:00–01:00
Safety: Pickpocketing risk increases in dense crowds. Use front-pocket wallet/money belt. Stay hydrated.
